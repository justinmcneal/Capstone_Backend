# CHAPTER 3 DATA-COLLECTION AND ADMINISTRATION GUIDE

**Project:** *MSME Pathways: A Smart Loan Support for Underserved Starting Entrepreneurs in the Informal Sector*  
**Status:** Draft for adviser and UREC/ethics review before administration

## 1. Basis in Chapters 1 and 2
- **Objective 1:** Identify the demographic profile, financial behaviors, challenges, and technological needs of underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector.
- **Objective 2:** Determine the system features and functionalities based on identified user needs and challenges.
- **Objective 3:** Evaluate the system in terms of functionality, usability, clarity of financial guidance, perceived usefulness, and user confidence through system testing and user feedback.
- **Sampling:** Purposive sampling.
- **Participants:** The final number of microentrepreneurs and lending representatives must be confirmed with the research adviser and reflected consistently in Table 1 and the approved ethics documents.
- **Locale:** PHINMA University of Pangasinan, Dagupan City, and nearby areas.
- **Methods:** Semi-structured interviews, questionnaires, non-participant observation, technical testing, and triangulation.

The current manuscript says that a structured post-use survey will be administered through Google Forms. A Google Form is only the delivery mechanism. The study still needs the questions, response scale, administration procedure, and tally method supplied by this packet.

## 3. Required Approval Before Data Collection

Before inviting participants:

1. Ask the adviser to review the questions, scales, sample, and planned analysis.
2. Determine whether the existing UREC/ethics submission covers these instruments. Submit an amendment if required.
3. Use the correct approved adult consent form. Use parental permission and minor assent only if minors are intentionally and ethically included; the target population described in Chapters 1–2 is normally adult entrepreneurs and lending personnel.
4. Confirm the number of respondents required by the course and approved research design.

## 4. Participant Recruitment

Apply the purposive criteria stated in Chapter 2.

### Microentrepreneur inclusion criteria

- Is at least 18 years old, unless a separate approved minor-participant protocol applies.
- Owns, operates, or is actively starting an informal or microenterprise.
- Is located in Dagupan City or a nearby approved study area.
- Is willing to participate voluntarily and provide informed consent.
- Can interact with the prototype using the provided test device or their own device.

### Lending representative inclusion criteria

- Has current or recent experience in loan processing, borrower assessment, microfinance, cooperative lending, or a similar role.
- Is willing to evaluate the officer-facing prototype using a test account and synthetic data.
- Provides informed consent.

Do not recruit subordinates, students, or financially vulnerable individuals through coercion. Participation, withdrawal, or refusal must not affect access to services, grades, employment, or future loan decisions.

## 5. Recommended Session Flow

Each controlled session may take approximately 60–120 minutes, consistent with the duration already stated in the consent materials.

### A. Microentrepreneur session

1. Explain the study and obtain signed informed consent.
2. Assign a respondent code such as `ME-01`; do not place the participant's name on response or tally sheets.
3. Administer the respondent profile form.
4. Conduct and document the semi-structured interview.
5. Give the participant a standard orientation that explains the prototype's educational nature.
6. Let the participant complete the customer tasks in the observation checklist using a test account and synthetic information.
7. Do not coach unless the participant asks for help or cannot continue. Record every assistance event.
8. Administer the microentrepreneur usability questionnaire.
9. Ask for final comments, remind the participant that the system does not guarantee approval, and explain how study data will be protected.

### B. Loan-officer/lending-representative session

1. Explain the study and obtain signed informed consent.
2. Assign the code `LO-01` or the next anonymous code.
3. Administer the applicable parts of the respondent profile form.
4. Provide a test officer account and synthetic borrower/application records.
5. Let the participant complete the officer tasks in the observation checklist.
6. Administer the loan-officer evaluation form.
7. Conduct a brief follow-up discussion about usefulness, missing information, workflow fit, risks, and recommendations.

## 6. Standard Prototype Conditions

Use the same conditions for each participant as far as practical:

- Same prototype version or record the exact version/build used.
- Same scripted tasks and task order.
- Same test data or equivalent synthetic test records.
- Same introductory instructions.
- Same device where possible, or record device differences.
- Record date, duration, interrupted tasks, technical failures, and assistance.
- Never ask participants to upload real government IDs, financial documents, passwords, OTPs, wallet keys, or other unnecessary sensitive information during testing.

If the prototype fails during a task, record the failure. Do not silently mark the task successful or coach the participant around the issue without documenting it.

## 7. Google Forms Setup

Separate the forms by purpose:

1. **Microentrepreneur Profile Form** — copy `01_RESPONDENT_PROFILE_FORM.md`.
2. **Post-Use Usability Form** — copy `04_MICROENTREPRENEUR_USABILITY_QUESTIONNAIRE.md`.
3. **Loan-Officer Evaluation Form** — copy `05_LOAN_OFFICER_EVALUATION_FORM.md`.

Recommended Google Forms settings:

- Do not automatically collect email addresses.
- Do not ask for names, exact home addresses, account numbers, passwords, OTPs, or wallet credentials.
- Use the assigned respondent code as the identifier.
- Do not allow public access to response spreadsheets.
- Restrict editing and download permissions to approved researchers.
- State the consent/approval reference and privacy notice at the beginning.
- Export a de-identified working copy for analysis.

The semi-structured interview and observation checklist should normally be researcher-administered rather than treated as a self-administered survey.

## 8. Data Quality and Triangulation

Use the following evidence together:

- Interview themes explain participant needs and concerns.
- Profile data describe the participants included in the controlled study.
- Task observations show whether participants could complete the intended workflows.
- Usability ratings show perceived clarity, ease, usefulness, trust, and confidence.
- Automated tests show whether executed technical cases passed.

Do not use one source to claim something it did not measure. For example, automated tests do not prove that users understood the interface, while three user ratings do not prove production scalability.

## 9. Minimum Evidence to Retain

Retain only as permitted by the approved protocol:

- Blank and approved instruments.
- Consent records stored separately from response data.
- Anonymous response sheets or exports.
- Interview notes/transcripts using respondent codes.
- Observation sheets.
- Survey tally and calculation sheet.
- Prototype version and test date.
- De-identified screenshots of results and technical test reports.
- Instrument/adviser validation record.

Do not place raw identifying information in Chapter 3 or public appendices.
