# PROPOSED CHAPTER 2 METHODOLOGY UPDATE

**Project:** *MSME Pathways: A Smart Loan Support for Underserved Starting Entrepreneurs in the Informal Sector*  
**Status:** Draft replacement/additional text for adviser and UREC/ethics review

This text is intended to correct the gap between the current Chapter 2 methodology and the Chapter 3 instruments. Do not insert it into the final manuscript until the adviser confirms the sample, instruments, scales, and ethics requirements.

## Proposed Addition: Research Instruments

### Research Instruments

The study will use a respondent profile form, a semi-structured interview guide, a pre-interaction and post-interaction assessment, role-specific post-use evaluation questionnaires, a non-participant observation and task checklist, and automated technical test records. The instruments are aligned with the research questions and objectives and are designed to combine qualitative descriptions of participant needs with quantitative and observational evidence from controlled prototype use.

**Respondent Profile Form.** The respondent profile form will collect limited, non-identifying characteristics relevant to the study. For microentrepreneurs, these include age group, optional gender, educational level, business stage, type of enterprise, borrowing experience, primary device, internet access, and comfort with mobile applications. For the lending representative, the form will collect the type of lending organization, range of professional experience, and general lending responsibility. Exact addresses, account credentials, and unnecessary financial identifiers will not be collected. The profile results will describe only the approved participants and will not be used to identify individuals.

**Semi-Structured Interview Guide.** The interview guide will be administered to the informal-sector microentrepreneurs before prototype evaluation. It contains open-ended questions concerning previous borrowing experiences, barriers to formal lending, confusing loan information, methods of evaluating loan risk, preferred guidance, willingness to provide information, desired system features, privacy and trust concerns, and recommendations. Follow-up probes may be used to clarify an answer without suggesting a preferred response. The responses will support the identification of financial behaviors, challenges, and technological needs.

**Pre-Interaction and Post-Interaction Assessment.** The same microentrepreneurs will complete an assessment immediately before and after interacting with the prototype. The instrument contains an eight-item loan-understanding check and five self-rated readiness and confidence statements. The knowledge items cover total repayment, comparison of loan offers, clarification of uncertain terms, late-payment consequences, credential safety, the limits of pre-qualification, application preparation, and the limitations of AI guidance. The repeated administration will provide descriptive evidence of change within the controlled session. Because the study uses a small purposive sample and has no control group, the change scores will not be interpreted as broadly generalizable or conclusive causal evidence.

If the pre-interaction assessment is not approved or cannot be administered before exposure to the system, the researchers will not claim measured improvement. In that case, the third objective and research question will be revised with adviser approval to evaluate perceived usability, clarity, usefulness, and confidence after prototype use.

**Microentrepreneur Post-Use Usability Questionnaire.** After completing the customer tasks, microentrepreneurs will answer a structured questionnaire covering ease of use and navigation, clarity of financial guidance, usefulness and relevance, and trust, privacy, and confidence. Items will use a five-point agreement scale ranging from 1, Strongly Disagree, to 5, Strongly Agree. A Not Applicable option will be available for features that were not included in the participant's approved test session. Open-ended questions will ask which feature was most helpful, which part was difficult or unclear, and which improvement was most important.

**Loan-Officer or Lending-Representative Evaluation Form.** The supporting lending respondent will complete a role-specific questionnaire after reviewing synthetic borrower and application records through the officer-facing prototype. The items will cover information organization, missing-information indicators, document and readiness presentation, preliminary decision support, workflow functionality, usability, traceability, security, and operational relevance. Open-ended questions will collect professional comments on missing information, workflow changes, risks, and future improvements. If only one lending representative participates, the result will be presented as an individual stakeholder assessment rather than a population-level evaluation.

**Non-Participant Observation and Task Checklist.** During each controlled prototype session, a researcher will observe the participant without performing the tasks on the participant's behalf. Standard customer or officer tasks will be classified as independently completed, completed with assistance, unsuccessful, blocked by a system or environment problem, or not included. The researcher will document assistance, navigation difficulty, misunderstood labels, displayed errors, spontaneous comments, and technical failures. Test accounts and synthetic information will be used to avoid collecting unnecessary real financial documents or credentials.

**Automated Technical Test Records.** Existing test reports will be used to summarize executed functional, authorization, security, AI-safety, loan-workflow, notification, analytics, and blockchain-related cases. Dated test results will be reported with their passed, failed, and skipped counts. Skipped tests will not be treated as passing evidence, and local or mocked test results will not be presented as proof of production deployment performance.

## Proposed Addition: Instrument Validation

### Validation of the Research Instruments

Before administration, the instruments will be reviewed by the research adviser and, where required, subject or methods specialists and the appropriate university research-ethics body. Reviewers will examine whether each item is clear, relevant, necessary, non-leading, understandable to the target participants, and aligned with the research objectives. Filipino and English wording will be compared for consistent meaning. Revisions will be documented before the approved versions are administered.

If permitted, the researchers may conduct a pilot with individuals who meet similar characteristics but are not part of the final participant group. The pilot will check instructions, completion time, language clarity, form logic, and prototype-task feasibility. Pilot responses will not be combined with final study results unless the protocol explicitly permits this. Any material post-approval change to the instruments or participant procedure will be submitted for the required adviser or ethics review before use.

## Proposed Replacement: Data-Gathering Procedure

### Data-Gathering Procedure

Data collection will occur in three phases after approval of the protocol and instruments.

**Phase 1: Requirements and Needs Assessment.** Eligible microentrepreneurs will be recruited through purposive sampling. After informed consent, each participant will receive an anonymous respondent code and complete the profile form. A researcher will then conduct the semi-structured interview in the participant's preferred approved language. With separate permission, an interview may be audio-recorded; otherwise, written notes will be used. Interview responses will be de-identified and coded to identify financial behaviors, barriers, information needs, desired system features, and privacy or usability concerns.

**Phase 2: Controlled Microentrepreneur Prototype Evaluation.** Each microentrepreneur will complete the pre-interaction assessment before receiving the standard prototype orientation. The participant will then perform the approved customer tasks using a test account and synthetic information while a researcher completes the non-participant observation checklist. Assistance will be provided only when requested or when the participant cannot continue, and each assistance event will be recorded. Immediately after the tasks, the participant will complete the post-interaction assessment and usability questionnaire and provide optional open-ended feedback.

**Phase 3: Lending-Representative Prototype Evaluation.** The lending representative will review the officer-facing workflow using a test account and synthetic borrower/application information. A researcher will record task outcomes and observed issues using the officer section of the observation checklist. The participant will then complete the loan-officer evaluation form and provide professional comments concerning workflow relevance, information quality, traceability, risks, and recommended improvements.

The researchers will use the same approved prototype version, standard task script, test records, and introductory instructions for each participant as far as practical. The date, device, network condition, session duration, assistance, technical interruption, and prototype version will be documented. The complete session may take approximately one to two hours, consistent with the approved consent information.

## Proposed Replacement: Treatment of Data

### Treatment of Data

The collected data will be organized and analyzed using descriptive and thematic techniques appropriate to the small purposive sample and controlled prototype evaluation.

**Profile Data.** Frequencies and percentages will summarize the participant group and applicable profile categories. Percentages will use the number of valid responses for the relevant group as the denominator. Categories that apply only to microentrepreneurs or only to the lending representative will not be combined.

\[
\text{Percentage}=\frac{f}{N}\times100
\]

where \(f\) is the category frequency and \(N\) is the number of valid responses in the relevant group.

**Interview Data.** Interview notes or approved transcripts will be reviewed and assigned initial codes. Related codes will be grouped into themes corresponding to borrowing behavior, formal-loan barriers, confusing information, preferred guidance, acceptable information sharing, desired system features, privacy and trust concerns, and recommendations. The researchers will retain both recurring and conflicting views. Each final theme will be linked to anonymous respondent codes and a frequency, but interpretation will prioritize context because of the small sample.

**Technological-Need Priority.** Identified technological needs will be classified as high, medium, or low priority. A need will be considered high priority when it is mentioned by most relevant participants, blocks a core task, or is required for safety or security. Medium-priority needs materially support a core workflow and are mentioned by some respondents. Low-priority needs are isolated preferences, optional enhancements, or future-scope suggestions. The source of each priority decision will be recorded.

**Usability and Lending-Representative Ratings.** Five-point items will be summarized using item means and dimension means. Ratings will be assigned numerical values from 1 for Strongly Disagree to 5 for Strongly Agree. Not Applicable responses will be excluded from the denominator and reported through the valid response count.

\[
\bar{x}=\frac{\sum fx}{N}
\]

where \(x\) is the numerical rating, \(f\) is its frequency, and \(N\) is the number of valid ratings. The verbal-interpretation intervals will be finalized with adviser approval and applied consistently. If only one lending representative participates, that person's ratings and comments will be reported descriptively and will not be treated as a population estimate.

**Pre-Interaction and Post-Interaction Results.** One point will be awarded for every correct knowledge item. For each participant, knowledge change will be calculated by subtracting the pre-interaction score from the post-interaction score. Confidence change will be calculated by subtracting the pre-interaction confidence mean from the post-interaction confidence mean. Group pre means, post means, and descriptive changes will be reported. The study will not claim statistical significance or broad causation from the proposed three-participant, one-session design.

**Observation Results.** Each applicable task will be classified as independent success, assisted success, unsuccessful, or system-blocked. Independent and assisted successes will be reported separately. A descriptive combined completion rate may be calculated as follows:

\[
\text{Completion Rate}=\frac{\text{Independent Successes + Assisted Successes}}{\text{Applicable Tasks - System-Blocked Tasks}}\times100
\]

System-blocked tasks will be reported as defects or environment limitations rather than participant failures.

**Technical Test Results.** Automated evidence will be summarized by test scope, date, passed count, failed count, skipped count, and limitation. A pass rate may be reported for executed tests only when the exact denominator is clear. Skipped, deselected, or unexecuted cases will be reported separately.

**Triangulation.** Interview themes, profile information, task observations, questionnaire ratings, pre/post changes, open feedback, and automated technical evidence will be compared. Convergent results will strengthen a finding, while contradictory results will be reported and examined rather than removed. Requirements and implemented features will be connected through a traceability table showing the source need, system response, and validation evidence.

## Proposed Addition: Data Protection During Analysis

Consent records will be stored separately from study responses. Working datasets will use anonymous respondent codes. Chapter 3 will present aggregated results and non-identifying comments. Access to raw responses will be limited to authorized researchers under the approved protocol. The researchers will not collect or expose passwords, OTPs, wallet private keys, unnecessary exact addresses, or real financial documents during prototype evaluation. Retention, deletion, sharing, and publication will follow the approved consent form, university requirements, and applicable privacy obligations.
