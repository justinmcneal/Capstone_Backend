Chapter 1 
INTRODUCTION 
Background of the Study 
Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) are universally acknowledged as a fundamental pillar of economic growth, employment generation, and poverty reduction in both developed and developing economies. On a global scale, MSMEs represent approximately 90% of all businesses and contribute to more than half of total employment worldwide (World Bank, 2023). Their role extends beyond economic contribution; MSMEs foster innovation, stimulate local markets, and serve as critical entry points for entrepreneurship, particularly among low- and middle-income populations. Despite their importance, MSMEs—especially those operating within the informal sector—continue to face systemic barriers that hinder their growth and sustainability, with limited access to formal financial services being among the most pressing challenges. While MSMEs generally face such barriers, those in the starting or early-stage phase face unique, heightened barriers because they lack the operational track record required by traditional lending institutions.
Access to finance is widely recognized as a key enabler of enterprise development. Formal credit allows MSMEs to invest in inventory, adopt new technologies, expand operations, and manage cash flow volatility.
However, many microentrepreneurs remain excluded from traditional banking systems due to rigid lending requirements such as collateral, formal income documentation, and established credit histories. This exclusion is especially acute for starting entrepreneurs, who face a "catch-22" situation: they lack the formal credit history necessary to secure initial capital, leaving them perpetually underserved. Informal-sector operators, who often rely on daily cash transactions and lack standardized financial records, are disproportionately affected by these constraints. As a result, they are frequently compelled to seek financing from informal lenders who impose excessive interest rates, short repayment periods, and exploitative terms, thereby increasing financial vulnerability and limiting long-term business growth.
In the Philippine context, the importance of MSMEs mirrors global trends but is even more pronounced. Data from the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) indicates that MSMEs account for 99.5% of all registered enterprises in the country and provide employment to approximately 63% of the national workforce (DTI, 2023). These enterprises are concentrated primarily in the microenterprise category, which includes small-scale vendors, sari-sari store owners, transport operators, and home-based entrepreneurs. Within this category, underserved starting entrepreneurs represent the most vulnerable segment, as they often remain informal in nature and operate outside regulatory and financial frameworks. 
The issue of financial exclusion remains a significant national concern. According to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Financial Inclusion Survey (2022), nearly 29% of Filipino adults do not possess formal financial accounts, and an even larger percentage lack access to formal credit facilities. Informal-sector workers are among the most affected due to factors such as insufficient financial literacy, a lack of trust in financial institutions, the perceived complexity of banking processes, and a fear of loan rejection. These challenges are further compounded by limited awareness of available financial products and inadequate support mechanisms to prepare borrowers for responsible loan participation. 
At the regional and local levels, the informal economy is particularly prevalent in densely populated urban centers and commercial districts where microentrepreneurs engage in daily retail, food vending, and service provision. While these activities generate consistent cash flow, they are rarely documented, making it difficult for financial institutions to assess creditworthiness using conventional evaluation methods. Additionally, existing microfinance outreach programs often rely on labor-intensive, manual screening processes that increase operational costs and limit scalability. As a result, many potentially creditworthy microentrepreneurs remain underserved or excluded from formal financial systems entirely. 
The rapid advancement and widespread adoption of mobile technology present a transformative opportunity to address these longstanding challenges. With near-universal smartphone ownership in the Philippines (Meltwater & We Are Social, 2025), mobile platforms have emerged as accessible and scalable tools for delivering financial services, education, and support to previously marginalized populations. Mobile-first solutions reduce geographic barriers, lower operational costs, and enable continuous user engagement. However, many existing digital financial applications are not tailored to the realities of informal-sector users, as they often assume stable income records, prior financial knowledge, and consistent internet connectivity. 
Empirical literature highlights the effectiveness of technology‑enabled interventions in promoting financial inclusion, and research indicates that digital financial literacy and digital financial tools are key components of improving financial knowledge and behaviors in the digital environment (Koskelainen, Kalmi, Scornavacca, & Vartiainen, 2023). Furthermore, the expansion of digital financial services and FinTech innovations has enabled the use of alternative data sources—including mobile phone usage patterns, digital transaction histories, and online behavioral indicators—to assess creditworthiness among populations excluded from traditional credit bureaus. Recent evidence demonstrates that these technology-driven approaches can significantly improve financial inclusion by providing more accessible and inclusive credit assessment mechanisms for underserved microenterprises and informal-sector operators (World Bank, 2022). Additionally, blockchain technology has emerged as a promising tool for enhancing transparency, security, and trust in financial transactions. Its decentralized and immutable architecture provides a reliable mechanism for recording and verifying financial activities, with applications in financial services that improve efficiency, reduce fraud, and enhance transparency (Wang, Xie, & Zhang, 2021). 
These findings emphasize the potential of intelligent, data-driven, and blockchain-integrated systems to bridge the gap between informal microentrepreneurs and formal financial institutions. 
Given these conditions, there is a clear and urgent need for an integrated, user-centered solution that combines financial education, ethical loan pre-screening, alternative data profiling, and secure transaction recording in a manner that is accessible to informal-sector microentrepreneurs. Addressing financial inclusion requires not only access to credit but also the development of financial capability, preparedness, and trust among borrowers through transparent and verifiable systems.
In response to this need, the project titled MSME Pathways: A Smart Loan Support for the Informal Sector is framed as a solution tailored specifically to underserved starting entrepreneurs. The AI-enabled, blockchain-integrated, mobile-first platform is designed to provide the initial guidance and readiness tools these entrepreneurs need to transition toward formal financial engagement. The system aims to enhance financial literacy, support responsible loan readiness, ensure transaction integrity through blockchain technology, and provide ethical, data-informed insights that can facilitate engagement with formal lending institutions. By leveraging mobile technology, intelligent analytics, and distributed ledger technology, the project seeks to contribute to inclusive economic growth and support the long-term sustainability of starting MSMEs in the informal sector of the Philippines.
Statement of the Problem
Underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector in the Philippines face significant barriers to accessing formal financial services, resulting in persistent financial exclusion and a reliance on informal lending sources with unfavorable terms. These challenges are particularly acute for those in the initial stages of their business journey, stemming from limited financial literacy, the absence of a formal credit history, distrust of financial institutions, and the perceived complexity of loan application processes. 
This study seeks to address the following research questions: 
1. What are the demographic profile, financial behaviors, challenges, and technological needs of underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector?
2. What system features and functionalities should be incorporated into the proposed AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System to support financial literacy and loan readiness?
3. How effective is the developed AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System in improving loan understanding, loan readiness, and user confidence among underserved starting entrepreneurs?
Objectives of the Study 
The objective of this study is to design, develop, and implement an AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System that provides accessible financial guidance, ethical loan pre-screening, and alternative data-driven profiling to support financial inclusion and improve access to formal lending opportunities for microentrepreneurs in the informal sector. 
Specifically, this study aims: 
1. To identify the demographic profile, financial behaviors, challenges, and technological needs of underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector.
2. To determine the system features and functionalities of the proposed AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System based on the identified user needs and challenges.
3. To evaluate the effectiveness of the system in improving financial understanding, loan readiness, and user confidence among underserved starting entrepreneurs through usability testing, functionality testing, and user feedback analysis.
Scope and Delimitation 
This study focuses on the design, development, implementation, and evaluation of MSME Pathways: A Smart Loan Support for the Underserved Starting Entrepreneurs in the Informal Sector, an AI-enabled system that aims to promote loan inclusion among underserved starting entrepreneurs in the Philippines. The proposed system provides AI-driven financial guidance through a conversational chatbot that explains loan concepts using simple and culturally relevant language. It also includes loan education modules covering topics such as interest rates, repayment terms, responsible borrowing, and loan risks to improve users' loan literacy and loan readiness.
The system incorporates alternative data profiling based on user-provided business information and self-reported financial activities to support loan readiness assessment and ethical loan pre-qualification using predefined criteria. Based on the profiling results, the system generates personalized feedback and recommendations to help users better prepare for formal loan applications. In addition, the system includes blockchain-based transaction recording using Ethereum-compatible smart contracts to promote transparency, integrity, and traceability of supported transactions within the system.
The primary users of the system are underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector, including sari-sari store owners, market vendors, home-based entrepreneurs, and other microentrepreneurs. The system also provides a web-based administrative interface for loan officers and system administrators to manage user profiles, review profiling results, monitor system activities, and generate basic reports. The mobile application is developed using Flutter for Android and iOS platforms, while the web-based administrative interface is built using Python, the Django framework, and a cloud-hosted MongoDB Atlas database.
This study is delimited to the development of a prototype-level smart loan support system. The system does not process or disburse actual loans through regulated financial institutions, integrate with live banking systems, payment gateways, or credit bureaus, nor perform official credit scoring or loan approval decisions. The AI-generated recommendations and loan readiness assessments are intended solely for educational and decision-support purposes and should not be interpreted as official financial or lending advice.
Furthermore, the performance of the AI components depends on the quality and representativeness of the available training and testing data. The system is evaluated within a controlled academic environment involving a limited number of participants over a defined evaluation period. Constraints related to time, resources, and available infrastructure limit large-scale deployment, extensive optimization, and enterprise-level scalability. The findings of this study are therefore limited to the implementation and evaluation of the proposed prototype and may not be generalized to all financial institutions or lending environments.


Significance of the Study
This study is significant because it contributes to the development of an AI-enabled smart loan support system that promotes loan inclusion among underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector. By providing accessible loan guidance, loan readiness assessment, and ethical alternative data profiling, the study aims to support more informed borrowing decisions while serving as a foundation for future innovations in financial technology. The findings and the developed system are expected to benefit the following stakeholders:
To the Underserved Starting Entrepreneurs. The primary beneficiaries of this study are underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector, including sari-sari store owners, market vendors, home-based entrepreneurs, and other microentrepreneurs at the early stages of their business journey. The proposed system provides simplified explanations of loan concepts, loan guidance, and loan readiness assessments that may help users better understand formal lending processes, identify areas for improvement, and make more informed borrowing decisions. By increasing loan awareness and preparedness, the system may encourage greater confidence in accessing formal loan services.
To the Microfinance Institutions and Loan Officers. The proposed system provides structured borrower information through alternative data profiling and loan readiness assessments, which may assist loan officers in conducting preliminary evaluations of prospective borrowers. Although the system does not replace institutional credit evaluation or lending decisions, it may serve as a complementary decision-support tool that promotes consistency, transparency, and efficiency during the initial assessment process.
To the Policymakers and Development Organizations. Government agencies, non-governmental organizations, and development institutions that promote loan inclusion may use the findings of this study as a reference in developing programs and initiatives that support underserved entrepreneurs. The proposed system demonstrates how emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and blockchain can be applied to address barriers to financial access in the informal sector.
To the Researchers and Project Developers. This study provides the researchers with practical experience in the design, development, implementation, and evaluation of an AI-enabled financial technology system. It strengthens their knowledge and skills in software engineering, mobile application development, artificial intelligence, blockchain technology, cloud computing, database management, and ethical system design while addressing a real-world social and economic challenge.
To the Academic Community and Future Researchers. This study contributes to the growing body of knowledge on the application of artificial intelligence, blockchain technology, alternative data profiling, and mobile technologies in financial inclusion. The documented methodology, system architecture, implementation, and evaluation results may serve as valuable references for future researchers and developers undertaking related studies or developing similar financial technology solutions.
Definition of Terms 
The following terms are defined operationally and conceptually to enhance understanding and precision within the context of this study:  
Alternative Data. Non-traditional information used to assess an individual's financial behavior and creditworthiness, such as business activity indicators, utility payment patterns, and self-reported income, rather than formal credit bureau records.  
Artificial Intelligence (AI). A branch of computer science focused on creating systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, including learning, reasoning, and natural language understanding. In this study, AI powers the financial guidance of chatbot and profiling mechanisms.  
Blockchain. A decentralized, distributed digital ledger technology that records transactions across multiple computers in a manner that ensures the records cannot be altered retroactively. In this study, blockchain is used to securely record and verify transaction references and user verification events.  
Chatbot. A software application that simulates human conversation through text-based or voice-based interactions. In this study, the chatbot provides financial guidance and answers user queries about loan concepts.  
Financial Inclusion. The availability and accessibility of useful and affordable financial products and services to individuals and businesses, particularly those traditionally excluded from formal financial systems. 
Financial Literacy. The ability to understand and effectively use various financial skills, including personal financial management, budgeting, and understanding loan terms and conditions.  
Loan Pre-qualification. A preliminary assessment process that evaluates an individual's basic eligibility for a loan based on self-reported information, without a formal credit check or commitment from a lender.  
MSME (Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprise). A classification of business entities based on asset size and number of employees. In the Philippines, micro enterprises have assets of up to PHP 3 million, small enterprises have assets between PHP 3 million and PHP 15 million, and medium enterprises have assets between PHP 15 million and PHP 100 million.  
Smart Contract. A self-executing program stored on a blockchain that automatically enforces the terms of an agreement when predetermined conditions are met. In this study, smart contracts developed using Solidity are used to record critical transaction references and verification events.  
Solidity. A high-level, object-oriented programming language used to write smart contracts on Ethereum-compatible blockchain platforms. In this study, Solidity is used to develop blockchain components for secure transaction logic.

Addressing financial inclusion requires not only access to credit but also the development of financial capability, preparedness, and trust among borrowers through transparent and verifiable systems.
In response to this need, the project titled MSME Pathways: A Smart Loan Support for the Informal Sector is framed as a solution tailored specifically to underserved starting entrepreneurs. The AI-enabled, blockchain-integrated, mobile-first platform is designed to provide the initial guidance and readiness tools these entrepreneurs need to transition toward formal financial engagement. The system aims to enhance financial literacy, support responsible loan readiness, ensure transaction integrity through blockchain technology, and provide ethical, data-informed insights that can facilitate engagement with formal lending institutions. By leveraging mobile technology, intelligent analytics, and distributed ledger technology, the project seeks to contribute to inclusive economic growth and support the long-term sustainability of starting MSMEs in the informal sector of the Philippines.
Statement of the Problem
Underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector in the Philippines face significant barriers to accessing formal financial services, resulting in persistent financial exclusion and a reliance on informal lending sources with unfavorable terms. These challenges are particularly acute for those in the initial stages of their business journey, stemming from limited financial literacy, the absence of a formal credit history, distrust of financial institutions, and the perceived complexity of loan application processes. 
This study seeks to address the following research questions: 
1. What are the demographic profile, financial behaviors, challenges, and technological needs of underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector?
2. What system features and functionalities should be incorporated into the proposed AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System to support financial literacy and loan readiness?
3. How effective is the developed AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System in improving loan understanding, loan readiness, and user confidence among underserved starting entrepreneurs?
Objectives of the Study 
The objective of this study is to design, develop, and implement an AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System that provides accessible financial guidance, ethical loan pre-screening, and alternative data-driven profiling to support financial inclusion and improve access to formal lending opportunities for microentrepreneurs in the informal sector. 
Specifically, this study aims: 
1. To identify the demographic profile, financial behaviors, challenges, and technological needs of underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector.
2. To determine the system features and functionalities of the proposed AI-enabled Smart Loan Support System based on the identified user needs and challenges.
3. To evaluate the effectiveness of the system in improving financial understanding, loan readiness, and user confidence among underserved starting entrepreneurs through usability testing, functionality testing, and user feedback analysis.
Scope and Delimitation 
This study focuses on the design, development, implementation, and evaluation of MSME Pathways: A Smart Loan Support for the Underserved Starting Entrepreneurs in the Informal Sector, an AI-enabled system that aims to promote loan inclusion among underserved starting entrepreneurs in the Philippines. The proposed system provides AI-driven financial guidance through a conversational chatbot that explains loan concepts using simple and culturally relevant language. It also includes loan education modules covering topics such as interest rates, repayment terms, responsible borrowing, and loan risks to improve users' loan literacy and loan readiness.
The system incorporates alternative data profiling based on user-provided business information and self-reported financial activities to support loan readiness assessment and ethical loan pre-qualification using predefined criteria. Based on the profiling results, the system generates personalized feedback and recommendations to help users better prepare for formal loan applications. In addition, the system includes blockchain-based transaction recording using Ethereum-compatible smart contracts to promote transparency, integrity, and traceability of supported transactions within the system.
The primary users of the system are underserved starting entrepreneurs in the informal sector, including sari-sari store owners, market vendors, home-based entrepreneurs, and other microentrepreneurs. The system also provides a web-based administrative interface for loan officers and system administrators to manage user profiles, review profiling results, monitor system activities, and generate basic reports. The mobile application is developed using Flutter for Android and iOS platforms, while the web-based administrative interface is built using Python, the Django framework, and a cloud-hosted MongoDB Atlas database.
This study is delimited to the development of a prototype-level smart loan support system. The system does not process or disburse actual loans through regulated financial institutions, integrate with live banking systems, payment gateways, or credit bureaus, nor perform official credit scoring or loan approval decisions. The AI-generated recommendations and loan readiness assessments are intended solely for educational and decision-support purposes and should not be interpreted as official financial or lending advice.
Furthermore, the performance of the AI components depends on the quality and representativeness of the available training and testing data. The system is evaluated within a controlled academic environment involving a limited number of participants over a defined evaluation period. Constraints related to time, resources, and available infrastructure limit large-scale deployment, extensive optimization, and enterprise-level scalability. The findings of this study are therefore limited to the implementation and evaluation of the proposed prototype and may not be generalized to all financial institutions or lending environments.

Definition of Terms 
The following terms are defined operationally and conceptually to enhance understanding and precision within the context of this study:  
Alternative Data. Non-traditional information used to assess an individual's financial behavior and creditworthiness, such as business activity indicators, utility payment patterns, and self-reported income, rather than formal credit bureau records.  
Artificial Intelligence (AI). A branch of computer science focused on creating systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, including learning, reasoning, and natural language understanding. In this study, AI powers the financial guidance of chatbot and profiling mechanisms.  
Blockchain. A decentralized, distributed digital ledger technology that records transactions across multiple computers in a manner that ensures the records cannot be altered retroactively. In this study, blockchain is used to securely record and verify transaction references and user verification events.  
Chatbot. A software application that simulates human conversation through text-based or voice-based interactions. In this study, the chatbot provides financial guidance and answers user queries about loan concepts.  
Financial Inclusion. The availability and accessibility of useful and affordable financial products and services to individuals and businesses, particularly those traditionally excluded from formal financial systems. 
Financial Literacy. The ability to understand and effectively use various financial skills, including personal financial management, budgeting, and understanding loan terms and conditions.  
Loan Pre-qualification. A preliminary assessment process that evaluates an individual's basic eligibility for a loan based on self-reported information, without a formal credit check or commitment from a lender.  
MSME (Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprise). A classification of business entities based on asset size and number of employees. In the Philippines, micro enterprises have assets of up to PHP 3 million, small enterprises have assets between PHP 3 million and PHP 15 million, and medium enterprises have assets between PHP 15 million and PHP 100 million.  
Smart Contract. A self-executing program stored on a blockchain that automatically enforces the terms of an agreement when predetermined conditions are met. In this study, smart contracts developed using Solidity are used to record critical transaction references and verification events.  
Solidity. A high-level, object-oriented programming language used to write smart contracts on Ethereum-compatible blockchain platforms. In this study, Solidity is used to develop blockchain components for secure transaction logic.

Development Methodology 


This section explains the development methodology that will be used in the study, including the selected software development model and the phases involved in system development, together with their corresponding deliverables.  


Software Development Model. The study will employ the Agile Software Development Model using the Scrum approach. Scrum is applied to support incremental delivery through short development cycles, enabling the research team to prioritize features, validate requirements, and continuously improve system components based on stakeholder feedback. This model is suitable for MSME Pathways because the proposed system includes complex and interdependent features such as AI-enabled guidance and profiling, as well as blockchain-based transaction integrity mechanisms, which require iterative refinement and staged integration.  
The Scrum approach is appropriate for the scope of the project because it encourages frequent feedback and progressive enhancement of the system’s functionality and usability. In the context of informal sector microentrepreneurs, user needs and varying of digital literacy, and practical concerns during lending transactions are best addressed through repeated validation of interface design, content clarity, and guidance accuracy. Scrum also supports risk reduction by enabling early testing of high-impact modules such as the AI chatbot and profiling logic, and by allowing blockchain components to be integrated and verified gradually to ensure system stability and reliability. 
 
 
Figure 1 


Phases of Scrum 
  
        Product Backlog and Backlog Refinement. The team will translate interview findings, survey inputs, and system requirements into user stories and will prioritize backlog items. Backlog refinement will be conducted regularly to clarify acceptance criteria, re-estimate effort, and re-order priorities based on technical feasibility and stakeholder feedback. Deliverables will include an updated Product Backlog, requirement mapping notes, and sprint-ready stories.  
Sprint Planning. At the start of each sprint, the team will select the highest-priority backlog items and will define a clear sprint goal aligned with research objectives. Planning outputs also included task decomposition, dependency identification, and assignment of implementation targets for frontend, backend, AI, and blockchain-related tasks. Deliverables will include the Sprint Backlog, sprint goal statement, and implementation task list.  
Sprint Execution (with Daily Scrum). During sprint execution, the team will hold short daily coordination sessions to report progress, identify blockers, and synchronize work. Core engineering activities will be performed within the sprint, including interface design refinement, feature development, API integration, validation checks, and defect fixing. Outputs will include working increments of mobile and web modules, backend services, AI functions, blockchain components, and corresponding test and issue records.  
Sprint Review. At the end of each sprint, completed increments will be demonstrated to stakeholder representatives for functional and usability validation. Feedback from the review will be documented and converted into backlog updates for the next sprint cycle to ensure continuous alignment with user and study requirements. Deliverables will include sprint review notes, accepted increments, and change requests.  
Sprint Retrospective. After the review, the team will conduct a retrospective to evaluate what worked well, what problems occurred, and what process improvements are needed. Agreed improvement actions will be carried forward to the next sprint to improve workflow efficiency, quality control, and collaboration. Deliverables will include retrospective findings and actionable process improvement items.  
Release and Deployment Preparation. After iterative sprint cycles, the team will consolidate validated increments and will prepare the prototype for controlled release and demonstration. Activities will include environment configuration, final integration and regression checks, documentation completion, and readiness verification for academic evaluation. Deliverables will include the prototype build, user manual, deployment notes, and demonstration checklist.  


System Architecture Overview 


The proposed system will follow a three-tier architecture consisting of the presentation layer, application layer, and data layer. This architecture enforces separation of concerns, allowing user interaction channels, business logic, and data services to evolve independently while remaining integrated through secure APIs.  


Figure 2 


Three-Tier Architecture 
  
        Presentation Layer. The presentation layer will include a Flutter-based mobile application for informal-sector microentrepreneurs and a web dashboard for loan officers and administrators. The mobile application supports onboarding, guidance access, profiling inputs, and loan-readiness interactions, while the web dashboard will support monitoring, application review, decision support, and reporting workflows.  
Application Layer. The application layer will be implemented using Python Django and Django REST Framework as the central service orchestration layer. It will manage authentication and role-based access, profile and document handling, AI-enabled chatbot and profiling logic, loan pre-qualification and lifecycle workflows, notifications, analytics, and blockchain transaction coordination through backend service integration.  
Data Layer. The data layer will consist of MongoDB Atlas for operational data storage, document storage services for uploaded files, and Ethereum smart contracts for tamper-resistant integrity records. MongoDB Atlas will store user, profile, loan, interaction, and audit data, while blockchain records will serve as immutable verification references. Sensitive personal data remains off-chain, and only transaction references are written to the blockchain.
